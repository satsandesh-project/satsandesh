# Interview consent — elder contextual interviews

**Owner:** M4 Sainathan
**Status:** v1 draft, 2026-09-22 — English master. Needs (1) supervisor
approval (`docs/research/ethics-approval-request.md`), (2) Telugu and Hindi
versions checked by a bilingual reader, (3) an audio recording of the
spoken script in each language before the first interview.
**Used with:** `docs/research/elder-interview-guide.md`, Part 1.

Design notes, so the form is not "simplified" back into legalese later:
large type when printed (≥ 16 pt); short sentences; every point is one
thing the elder can say yes or no to; read aloud, never handed over to read
alone; two separate decisions (take part / record audio); signature,
thumbprint, or witnessed spoken consent are all equal; the elder keeps a
copy.

---

## Part A — Spoken script (read aloud; also recorded as audio)

*Read at a normal pace. Pause after each paragraph and check the elder is
following. Use the elder's language.*

Namaskaram. My name is ______, and I am a student at ______. Four of us are
building a phone app for the ______ community, for sending voice messages
and joining satsang from home. We want it to work well for elders — so we
would like to learn from you.

Here is what we would like to do today. It will take about half an hour,
maybe forty-five minutes.

First, I will ask you some questions about how you use your phone now —
which apps, who you talk to, what is easy, what is difficult. There are no
right or wrong answers. If you do not use a phone much, that is also very
useful for us to know.

Then I will ask you to do a few small things on a phone while I watch — for
example, opening WhatsApp, playing a voice message, or scanning a QR code.
We are testing the *app*, not you. If something is difficult, that is the
app's fault, and it is exactly what we need to see. You can stop at any
point, or skip anything you do not want to do.

I will write notes as we go. I will not write your name on the notes — only
a number. I will not look at your messages, your photos, or anything else
on your phone, and I will not copy anything from it.

If you agree, I would also like to record the sound of our conversation, so
I can check my notes later. This is separate — you can say yes to the
interview and no to the recording. Any recording is deleted within one
month.

Your family member is welcome to sit with us. During the small tasks I will
ask them to stay quiet, so that we see how the app works for you.

Nothing you say today will be shared with your name attached. What we learn
will go into a report about how elders use phones, written so that no one
can tell who said what.

You can change your mind at any time — today, or later. Just tell me, or
tell ______ at the organisation, and we will remove your notes.

Do you have any questions? Would you like to take part?

## Part C — Interviewer's checklist (not read aloud)

Before:
- [ ] Consent form printed in the elder's language, large type, two copies
- [ ] Audio version of Part A available on the interviewer's phone in case
      the elder prefers to listen
- [ ] Participant code assigned on the supervisor's sheet — *the name goes on
      that sheet only*
- [ ] Interview guide Part 1 (session metadata) filled

During:
- [ ] Read Part A fully; do not paraphrase the recording or withdrawal points
- [ ] Both decisions recorded separately; recording starts only after the
      second "yes"
- [ ] Family member briefed to stay silent during tasks
- [ ] Any sign of distress → stop, offer to end, note it in Part 6 of the guide

After:
- [ ] Elder's copy handed over
- [ ] Notes carry the code only; no name, phone number or photo anywhere
- [ ] If recorded: file named by code, stored off-repo, deletion date
      written on the consent form
- [ ] Findings entered in the guide's Part 7/8 the same day

## Part D — Participant-code scheme

- Codes `E01` to `E08` for interviews; pilot participants continue the
  series (`E09`…) so one person keeps one code across both.
- One mapping sheet: `code | name | phone | consent date | recording? |
  deletion date | withdrawn?`. Held by the supervisor (or the liaison —
  `ethics-approval-request.md` §8 Q3). **Never in this repository, never
  in the app.**
- Anything committed here — consolidated findings, SRS §3 evidence, the
  final report — refers to codes or to counts ("6 of 8"), never to names.
